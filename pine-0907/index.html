Games

Your people gave the world chess. Legend says the Sasanian court answered with nard and sent it back as a riddle nobody could solve. Fifteen hundred years later, one Indian-American with a fresh PhD thinks he can beat a Persian at backgammon. Persia is not for beginners. Ready to lose? No cheating this time.

Nard: doubles & dares

Regular backgammon, bottle open, four extra rules. Best of three, the winner picks the movie.

  1. Roll doubles: other player drinks, or answers one honest question. Their pick.
  2. Get hit: one sip and one specific compliment, out loud.
  3. Gammon: loser owes a ten-minute massage tonight.
  4. Caught cheating: forfeit the game and any right to complain.

    Tuesday morning: thirty minutes on the rug

    No weights in the cabin, so breath and bodyweight. Couples yoga, six shapes, no experience needed.

    1. Back-to-back breathing, 3 min: sit cross-legged, spines touching, match breaths.
    2. Partner forward fold, 3 min each: sit facing, feet touching, hold wrists; one leans back, the other folds. Swap.
    3. Double downward dog, 2 min: one in down dog, the other walks their feet onto the first person's lower back. Laughing is part of the pose.
    4. Partner boat, 2 min: sit facing, hold hands, soles together, lift to a V. Engineers may discuss the moment balance.
    5. Seated twist, 2 min each side: back-to-back, twist toward each other, reach for the other's knee.
    6. Savasana, heads touching, 5 min: then you make breakfast.
